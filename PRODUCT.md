# Product

Written by Claude from the project conversation; the owner skipped the design
interview. Edit anything here that is wrong.

## Register

product

## Users

People looking for something to go to: concerts, games, shows. Mostly on a
phone, often in a hurry because good seats are going. The site was first shown
to a room of people using it at the same moment, so many visitors on one event
is the normal case, not the edge case. The UI never mentions a class or a demo.

## Product Purpose

A ticket site where the seat map is live: you see seats taken as it happens,
you hold yours for two minutes, and exactly one person ever gets a seat.
Listings mix PickTick's own events with real events from Ticketmaster.
Reservations are free and no real ticket is issued; the site says so once, at
checkout and on the ticket, without making a theme of it. The landing page is
the one brand-register surface; everything after it is product register.

## Brand Personality

Quick, plain-spoken, a little playful. It sounds like a box-office window: short
sentences, no hype. The interface gets out of the way of the seat map.

## Anti-references

- Real ticketing sites' dark patterns: fake urgency banners, surprise fees,
  pop-ups over the seat map.
- Neon gradients and glow effects. The theme is dark by the owner's choice, but flat: one saturated colour, no gradients.
- Ticketmaster's own look, logo or blue. PickTick is its own brand: velvet-rope magenta on near-black, a marquee wide grotesque.
- Dashboards that turn four counters into giant hero numbers.

## Design Principles

1. The seat map is the page. Everything else is subordinate to it.
2. Show the machinery: state changes made by other people are visible the
   moment they happen, with who did them.
3. Never rely on colour alone; a washed-out projector must still tell
   available, held, yours and sold apart.
4. Say what happened and what to do next, in one sentence, where it happened.
5. One thumb on a phone is the baseline; the projector view is the same page,
   wider.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Every seat is a labelled, keyboard-reachable control. Seat states
differ by shape and mark as well as colour. Motion only signals a state change
and is removed under reduced-motion. Countdown and conflict messages are
announced to screen readers.
